# Cross-sectional ranking + event-study model card (PR 8.3)

Research only. Never a buy/sell/hold recommendation. Nothing in
this card is a statement about any real market, symbol or event:
the evaluation ran entirely on synthetic data (see "Run" below),
and every verdict below is "no edge" / "no significant abnormal
return" — which is the expected outcome on random data, and is
published as it came out, not re-tuned away.

## Protocol

Pre-registered in `cross-sectional-eval-protocol.md` **before** this
evaluation ran: 20 synthetic symbols, 400 daily closes each from
seed 20261114, 12 monthly ranking dates, 21-day forward returns,
deciles 1–10 (10 = top), 4 ranking rules (momentum, value, quality,
composite) plus an equal-weight baseline, costs charged on every
rebalance (NEPSE sourced schedule at NPR 100,000 per name; global
flat cost_fraction 0.001 per leg), verdict by the mean net
top-minus-bottom spread with a 95% bootstrap CI (2000 resamples,
seed 20261114) — "beats zero" only if the mean spread is positive
AND the CI lies entirely above zero, else "no edge". Event study:
estimation window [-250, -31], event window [-5, +5], market-model
OLS, significant only if BOTH |t| > 1.96 AND bootstrap p < 0.05.
No parameter was tuned after seeing results. Reproduce with:

```text
python3 scripts/evaluate_cross_sectional.py
```

## Run

- **Run date:** 2026-11-14. **Data:** synthetic, generated by the
  script from seed 20261114 — geometric random walks (drift 0,
  daily volatility 2% per symbol, 1.5% for the market index) and
  synthetic annual fundamentals drawn independently of the price
  paths. **No factor structure is planted**: forward returns are
  independent of every proxy by construction, so the expected
  verdict on every ranking rule is "no edge" and the expected
  event-study outcome is "no significant abnormal return".
- **Ranking rules tried:** 4 (momentum 126-bar formation skipping
  the most recent 21 bars; value = mean z of earnings yield and
  book-to-price; quality = mean z of ROE and gross margin;
  composite = mean z of the three) plus the equal-weight baseline.
- **Events:** 8 synthetic events from
  `tests/fixtures/nepse_events_SYNTHETIC.csv` (6 NEPSE corporate
  actions: dividend, bonus, right, book closure; 2 global
  earnings), each on a 400-bar synthetic price series with the
  event at bar 280–350.
- **Floorsheet:** `tests/fixtures/nepse_floorsheet_SYNTHETIC.csv`
  (10 invented rows, 3 brokers, 2 symbols).
- **Implementation:** `toolkit/cross_sectional.py`,
  `toolkit/event_study.py`, `toolkit/nepse_floorsheet.py`
  (stdlib only).

## Results — decile spreads: no edge, all four rules, both cost models

Mean net top-minus-bottom spread across the 12 rebalances, with the
95% bootstrap CI. A positive mean is not an edge: the CI must clear
zero, and none does.

| Rule | Mean spread (NEPSE costs) | 95% CI | Mean spread (global costs) | 95% CI | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| momentum | +0.0188 | [−0.0291, +0.0656] | +0.0188 | [−0.0291, +0.0656] | **no edge** |
| value | +0.0333 | [−0.0211, +0.0819] | +0.0333 | [−0.0211, +0.0819] | **no edge** |
| quality | −0.0380 | [−0.0856, +0.0055] | −0.0380 | [−0.0856, +0.0055] | **no edge** |
| composite | +0.0204 | [−0.0286, +0.0665] | +0.0204 | [−0.0286, +0.0665] | **no edge** |

Equal-weight baseline, mean net 21-day forward return per
rebalance: **−0.0069** under NEPSE costs, **−0.0015** under global
costs.

**Why the two cost columns are identical for the spread (read
this before quoting the table):** both legs of a long-short spread
pay the same round-trip fraction, so the cost cancels exactly in
the difference — top net minus bottom net equals top gross minus
bottom gross. The costs were charged (0.74% round trip NEPSE,
0.20% global — see below), they just do not move a spread whose
legs cost the same. Costs DO bite the absolute returns: the
equal-weight baseline loses 0.54 points more per rebalance under
NEPSE costs than under global costs (−0.0069 vs −0.0015), which is
exactly the 0.54-point round-trip difference. A long-only decile
portfolio would feel the full charge; the spread does not. This is
arithmetic, not a finding — and it is why the card reports the
baseline alongside the spread.

**Costs charged, per rebalance per name:** NEPSE round trip 0.74%
of traded value (NPR 100,000: broker slab 0.33% = NPR 330 + SEBON
0.015% = NPR 15 + DP Rs 25 = NPR 370 per leg; CGT excluded — lot
gain / holding period not tracked, same exclusion as the portfolio
backtester). Global round trip 0.20% (explicit cost_fraction 0.001
per leg; no sourced US fee schedule exists, so the backtester's
UnknownCostError rule applies and an explicit fraction is required
rather than an assumed zero).

## Reading (and not over-reading) this

- **"No edge" is the result, not a failure to run.** The data was
  random by construction, so a "beats zero" verdict would have
  meant the pipeline or the seed was broken. The evaluation's job
  was to prove the pipeline charges costs, forms deciles, computes
  spreads and CIs, and labels a non-win as "no edge" — it did all
  of that, and the decision rule's other branch ("beats zero") is
  proven by the planted-spread positive control in the tests.
- **The CIs are wide for a reason:** 12 rebalances, 2 names per
  decile, 21-day forward returns on 2%-volatility random walks.
  Three of four mean spreads are positive and none clears zero —
  that is noise around zero, not a near-miss edge. Do not quote
  the +0.0333 value mean as "value almost works".
- **The bootstrap assumes independent rebalances:** the 12
  spreads overlap in calendar time (monthly dates, 21-day
  forwards), so the CI is likely too narrow. Treat the verdict as
  supported by the design (random data, no planted structure)
  rather than as a formal significance proof.
- **Explicit limits:** one synthetic window (400 bars), 20 invented
  symbols, one train/test shape (12 rebalances), closes only, no
  dividends/splits, no real fundamentals, no real events. The
  EDGAR bridge is tested on fixture facts only — no live EDGAR
  call was made for this card. This card makes NO claim about
  momentum, value or quality in any real market, about NEPSE
  corporate actions, or about any broker flow. A real-data run
  needs real user-supplied CSVs (NEPSE) or EDGAR fundamentals plus
  Yahoo prices (global), and its numbers would belong in a new
  card, not as an edit to this one.
- **No failure hidden:** all four ranking rules failed the
  protocol decision rule and are labelled "no edge" in the table
  above; the event study found no significant abnormal return.
  Had any rule passed, it would have been printed in the same
  table — on random data a pass would have been reported as a
  chance outcome, not an edge.
